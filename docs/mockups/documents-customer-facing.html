Why a quote is a different shape from an invoice. An invoice is a demand with a date on it: an amount, a due date, a way to pay. A quote is an offer, so it leads with what is proposed in words before it shows a price, states the day the price stops being true, offers more than one option so the reader has a choice rather than a decision, and ends with three ways to answer — accept, ask, or book — instead of one. The one thing it shares with the invoice is the money grid, because that is where a reader looks for the number.
The fields a quote would need to grow. Quote carries quoteNumber, companyId, contactId, title, status, subtotal, taxRate, taxTotal, total, notes, createdById, createdAt, updatedAt and lineItems. It has no expiry and no option grouping; it is the notes field that says “Valid for 30 days”, which a document should not have to parse.

One document language, four documents

The invoice, the quote, the ticket sheet and the statement share one letterhead, one meta line, one money grid, one totals block, one pay block, one footer and one set of status marks. A client who reads two of them is reading one product.

The audience decides the content

The ticket sheet is built from the visible comments; the file copy is the client copy plus a count. The invoice states its amount once. The quote offers a choice. The document says what it is and who it is for, on its own face.

Where a value is missing, it is said

No bank details, no jurisdiction column, no quote expiry, no client-facing payment page. Each is drawn with the field it would need named beside it, rather than filled in with something invented.

Why the two arrangements differ

Modern answers “which document, and what will it do” in one press: the rail carries the document, the band carries its facts, the sentence beside the button says who the email will go to, and the footer counts what resulted. Classic answers the same question as a form, because the classic interface is a form: the document is a labelled select, the copy and the paper size are fields you set before you act, the matches are a table with a heading, and the action is Save, not Print or save — a form is read once, top to bottom, and then submitted.

What they share is the state, the documents and the words — never the layout. useRedesign() decides which is drawn: localStorage.setItem("c7_ui_redesign","0") for the classic dialog, removeItem to return to the instance's default.
