Two lists that never said the thing they were opened to say

Contacts is 21 cards and a rail that repeats the card. Below, each screen is composed again out of the modern interface's own furniture: a rail of the choices it is made of, rows that lead with the person and their client, a sheet that does the work, a policy track read as three sentences and a number, and a table that says what four boards actually do.

Every figure was read from the running instance on 9 October 2026 — GET /api/clients · /api/clients/contacts · /api/tickets · /api/boards · /api/boards/metrics · /api/configuration — and cross-read in apps/api/src/routes/boards.ts (how the six tiles are counted), prisma/schema.prisma (ServiceBoard, Contact), Administration.tsx (the board editor), CloseTicketDialog.tsx and src/index.css. How a ticket on this board ends

NOC Alerts closes a ticket without emailing the client.

Nothing is sent. The note is still recorded on the ticket.

This is the answer the close dialog starts from, so nobody has to remember it at the moment they close something: the board's tickets arrive from monitoring systems at no-reply addresses, and a closure email to one of those is a message to nobody that looks like a notification. Choose Email the client and every close on this board mails the contact, with a reply reopening the ticket as Customer reopened.

The choice is the board's own setting — ServiceBoard.notifyCustomerOnClose, false for NOC Alerts and true for the other three — drawn as the setting's answer rather than as a caption under a title, because a caption is what the settings screen already does and nobody reads a board's policy off a grey line.

Making a board

Two fields and one decision: what it is called, what it is for, and whether closing one of its tickets emails the client. Everything else — SLA response and resolution, the code its numbers carry, auto-close days, the follow-up interval — is a later decision, made from the board's own page rather than from the form that creates it.

Email the client when a ticket on this board is closed

Turn this off for a board whose tickets arrive from monitoring systems: their addresses are no-reply, so the closure email reaches nobody. Closing a single ticket can still send one.

On by default, and off on exactly one board here. The dialog that creates a board already asks this question — the gap the reimagining closes is the other direction: the board's own page says the answer, so the two never disagree.

The create form in Administration.tsx posts only name, description and notifyCustomerOnClose, with the paragraph above written beside the checkbox — so the decision is already treated as belonging to the moment a board is defined. This screen keeps that and shows the same switch in the same words, so "where does a board's close behaviour come from" has one answer in two places.

What this board promises, in the order it happens

First response

30 minutes

The clock starts when the ticket is raised. Half an hour on this board, because what arrives here is an alert and not a request.

Resolved

4 hours

A resolution does not close the ticket: it waits for the client's word, and until that word arrives the ticket is still open on this page.

Follow-up mail

every 24 hours

While a ticket waits, a reminder goes out daily rather than piling up unremarked — followUpEnabled, followUpIntervalHours: 24.

Closed by itself

14 days

Fourteen days without a reply and it closes — and on this board it closes without emailing anybody, which is the decision at the top of the page.

Every board here: auto-close on, 14 days, follow-up every 24 hours Response and resolve differ: 30 min / 4 h here, 2 h / 8 h on Infrastructure, 4 h / 24 h on the other two

Minutes are the storage, not the sentence: the columns are slaResponseMinutes 30 and slaResolutionMinutes 240, and Administration prints "SLA 30 / 240 min". Half an hour and four hours are the same two numbers said out loud, which is the whole change — the field stays in the API and the board says what a person reads.

The follow-up figure is read from followUpIntervalHours, which holds 24. Administration currently prints "follow-up every 120 min" for the same board, because it reads followUpIntervalMinutes — a column ServiceBoard does not have, falling back to its own default of 120. That is a bug in the settings screen, not a difference of opinion, and this page reads the column that exists.
