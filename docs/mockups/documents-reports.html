Mockup — nothing changed Printable documents and PDFs · seven screens in one file · drawn in millimetres

Five families of document leave this product through four renderers. One sheet of paper should hold them.

The reports, the review packs and the banded designer's output are not three designs — they are one document wearing three renderers, and today each renderer decides the page for itself. This file writes the document language down once (screen one), then applies it: the real Aging Report (two), a real business review pack (three), the banded engine's own machinery with its bands made visible (four), the chooser nobody has built yet (five), and the audit of what that changes (six).

Every figure below was read from the running instance on 9 October 2026 — the API on :4000 and its database. Where a figure could not be read it is absent and the page says so. The invoice PDF and the ticket sheet are the companion file's, not this one's.

The document language — argued, then drawn

docs/mockups/documents-reports.html agreed with the companion file paper is not the interface
The document is not the interface. It is a third surface (DESIGN.md §8)

A report is printed, stapled, photocopied, scanned into somebody's finance folder and read in greyscale. So: the app's theme must not reach it (which is why the hex literals in reportKit.tsx, PageRenderer.tsx, .print-letterhead and .ticket-print-only are legitimate and listed in scripts/lint-design-tokens.mjs); no border-radius, no drop shadow, no card-inside-a-card; and every rule has to survive a 150dpi greyscale scan of a sheet that was printed on a printer low on cyan.

The ruleWhat it means when a page is drawn Why this and not the other thing
Paper first A4 and Letter; portrait for money and service documents, portrait and landscape for reports. Every sheet is drawn at true paper fidelity on a millimetre scale: A4 is 210mm × 297mm, the side margin is 18mm on every sheet size, and nothing is positioned in pixels. The 1:1 control at the top right of this file is that claim, testable. Today's PDF is drawn in pt on an A4 landscape page it never asks about (documentToPdf: { orientation: "landscape", unit: "pt", format: "a4" }), and the print window writes no @page size at all, so the browser decides the paper. A pixel grid can't tell you whether a table fits — millimetres can.
One accent Paper white, ink #0f172a, body #334155, muted #64748b, rules at #cbd5e1 / #e2e8f0 and a 0.6pt ink rule where a rule must declare something — and one accent: brand crimson #c00000, already DOCUMENT_BRAND.crimson and already the colour of the wordmark's 7 on every document this product makes. The companion file (documents-customer-facing.html, the invoice, the quote and the ticket sheet) uses the same names and the same numbers, because a report and an invoice are one language. The one thing I am arguing with in the brief. Today's letterhead rule and bar fill use DOCUMENT_BRAND.accent = #00c0f4, which measures 2.1:1 against white — under the 3:1 a non-text graphical object needs (DESIGN.md §7), and the first thing a low-cyan printer or a photocopier drops. Crimson measures 6.5:1 against paper and never vanishes in greyscale, and it makes the document carry one accent instead of two. Tone is also carried by weight — an accent rule is 1.4pt where a hairline is 0.5pt — so a mono reader gets the same signal from the stroke as a colour reader gets from the hue.
The letterhead The shield, the wordmark with its crimson 7, the document title, and a meta line: client · period · as of · generated at · who generated it. A hairline beneath. documentMetaLine already assembles subtitle, period and generated-at. It never says who — and "who made this number" is the first question asked of a report that is being disputed. The shield is redrawn as vector here because this file may load no external assets; /icon-192.png stays the source of truth.
The masthead 3–5 headline figures at the top of page one: 20pt tabular numerals, 8.5pt small-caps labels, tone carried by a 1.4pt accent rule above the figure, and a sentence under the row saying what the figures are measured over. A coloured slab is the interface's way of shouting; it is also the part of a document that photocopies to a grey rectangle and takes the black text with it. A rule carries the tone and costs no toner.
Rhythm and rules, not cards A 4.5mm baseline grid; 16mm top and 20mm bottom margins; hairlines and space to separate. A section heading is small caps over a hairline. Prose in one 174mm column; tables on a measured grid. The current printed documents are the screen's own .card grammar — rounded-xl, tinted tiles, zebra striping. That is precisely why they read as a screen printed out. Paper separates with a rule because that is what paper has.
Type in points One family (Inter, with the stack the config already names), JetBrains Mono for identifiers and invoice numbers. Title 15pt, section 9pt small caps, table body 9pt, headers 8.5pt, notes 8.5pt. Nothing below 8.5pt. Tabular numerals in every figure column, every total and every masthead figure. The kit's PDF goes to 6.8pt for a tile label and the print window to 9px — 6.8pt is below what survives a fax-quality scan, and 9px is not a unit anybody specified. In every figure column the digits must line up or a reader comparing two columns is reading ragged type instead of a table.
Tables A header rule; no zebra striping; figures right-aligned and tabular; a total row emphasised with a rule above it rather than a fill; and a column-selection rule — a table that fits the page beats one scaled to fit. documentToPdf sets alternateRowStyles to a grey, which on paper is the rectangle you get when a scanner dithers. A total needs to be findable by eye at a glance, and a rule above it is unambiguous at any contrast.
Charts and bars Vector shapes with the value written beside every bar, axis ticks at the ends, no legend, and the one bar that matters in the accent. Greyscale-safe by construction because the number is always present. The kit's bars are a rounded track with a coloured fill and the value at the right — already close. The failure it avoids: a chart whose meaning is only decodable in colour, printed onto a mono laser printer in a finance office.
Structure Page 1 = letterhead + masthead + the headline table. Continuation = a running head (document, period, page n of m) and a footer (instance, generated at, the confidential line). The document ends with the basis block. This repository's rule is that a report says why a number is not known rather than estimating it. The screens say it in prose; the print-out has to say it too, or the promise stops at the edge of the browser window. Page n of m already exists in the kit's footer — it is the running head that is missing.
Every page worth printing No page that is three lines and a page number. A table that would leave a tenth of a page empty gets its column selection widened or its rows allowed to reach the margin; a section that would sit alone is pulled back to the previous page. A two-page report where page two is a page number is a one-page report with a complaint. The real instance has two open invoices, and screen two has to be honest about that rather than fill the page.

The specimen says one thing that is not true of a real report

A real report puts the basis block last, on the last page — screen two draws it there. It is placed high on this sheet for one reason only: rule eleven needs something to point at. The other eleven markers point at exactly what a real page one carries.

What each numbered part is, and why

Twelve rules. The four that are new to this product's paper are 2, the accent in 4, 11 and the running head on the next screen.

1
The letterhead — the shield and the wordmark

The same mark on all five families, from documentBrand.ts. The wordmark's 7 keeps its crimson, and that crimson is then the document's only accent rather than a second colour appearing here.

2
The meta line, and the name that was missing

Client · period · as-of · generated at · who generated it. documentMetaLine builds the first three today; the generator is new, because the first question asked of a disputed figure is who produced it.

3
A hairline, not a coloured band

0.6pt of ink where the letterhead ends. Today's letterhead rule is 2pt of cyan, which is the heaviest thing on the page and says nothing.

4
The masthead, with tone in the rule

20pt tabular figures, 8.5pt small-caps labels. The two that need attention take a 1.4pt crimson rule above them; the others take the hairline. A colour-blind reader, a greyscale print and a photocopy all get the same signal because it is carried by the stroke's weight.

5
One sentence saying what the figures are measured over

Written from the endpoint's own basis.measure and basis.openStatuses, not composed by hand — so it cannot drift from the number it explains.

6
Sections as small caps over a hairline

9pt, letter-spaced, with the full-width hairline the section hangs from. Numbered, so a contents page in a pack can refer to them — screen three.

7
A header rule instead of a filled header

Today's documentToPrintWindow fills the header with near-black ink; the PDF does the same. A 0.6pt ink rule under the labels carries the same structure for a fraction of the toner and none of the risk of a dark rectangle photocopying into a black bar.

8
Figures right-aligned, tabular, no zebra

Every figure column is font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums and right-aligned, so the decimal points line up down the column and the eye can subtract two of them. The even-row tint is gone: alternateRowStyles on paper is dithering.

9
The total, marked by a rule above it

A total row is bold with a 0.5pt rule above and no fill. It is findable at a glance and it is legible in any contrast.

10
Bars with the value written beside them

Vector, hairline track, one bar in the accent: the one the sentence above names. A zero-length bar is drawn as an empty cell rather than omitted, so five bands always print as five bands and the reader can see that three of them are empty.

11
The basis block — including what cannot be known

The measured rule, the statuses counted, the currencies, and a row that names the three things this document cannot say: no period filter, no credit-terms field, and a population of two invoices. The repository's rule is that a report says why a number is not known rather than estimating it.

12
The footer, on every sheet

Instance · generated at by whom · the confidential line · page n of m. A single sheet that came loose in a photocopier tray still says what it is and whose it is.

What a cover carries, and why

Six facts and one sentence, in the order a person asks them: what period, against what, whose review, who prepared it, when, from which instance. Nothing else — no masthead figures, because a cover that opens with the numbers has given away the summary; no contents, because the contents is a page people turn to and a cover is a page people look at.

And it names the preparer, which no document this product makes does today.

Why the pack is paginated rather than continuous

documentToPrintWindow prints one continuous document and lets the browser break it wherever it lands, and documentToPdf adds a page when the cursor reaches a margin. Neither knows what a section is. So a review pack arrives with a table split under its own heading and the browser's own page-1-of-9 in the corner.

A section startsOn its own page, with a sentence and then its figures
A table breaksOnly between rows, never between a heading and its first row
A group breaksWith its heading repeated and continued said in the running head
A snapshotSays "as at 30 September 2026" on the page it is on
Page countCounted across the pack, not per section

None of that is new machinery. layoutReport already does all four of those things in millimetres for banded templates — screen four shows the bands. What the pack needs is for the generated document to be laid out the same way, instead of being flowed into whatever the browser decides.

Two real findings, and the one thing not to change

1. The group rule is cyan. The starter's group-header line is { width: 0.4, color: "#22d3ee" } — the same cyan that measured 2.1:1 against paper on screen one. It is the line that marks where a group ends, which is the single boundary a reader navigates by, so it is the worst place in the product to put the one colour a photocopier drops. Crimson, 0.6mm.

2. The page footer is 7.5pt. Below the 8.5pt floor, in the line that carries the page number and the generated date. 8.5pt, and it costs 0mm of band height because the band is 8mm tall with room.

What is not to change. The millimetre layout, the band heights, the repeat rules, the anchored footer and the jsPDF vector text are already right, and this file proposes no change to any of them. A custom report is also deliberately excluded from the letterhead — documentBrand.ts says why: putting the product's shield above bands an author placed by hand would be overruling them. The designer already offers the mark and the wordmark as placeable images (brand-mark, brand-wordmark in the catalogue), so the author who wants a letterhead places one. That stays.

And the band tints (BAND_BACKGROUNDS — pale cyan for a group header, pale teal for its footer) stay on the canvas: they are an overlay in PageRenderer.tsx drawn only when showBandTint is on, and the printed sheet is white because a band carries no background of its own. Paper gets rules; the canvas gets tints.

What the panel is allowed to promise, and what it must not

The answerWhat the panel saysWhat it must not say
Print "Two sheets, A4 portrait, at the sizes this document is designed in." Anything about the reader's printer. The sheets print at their own size; the printer's "fit to page" is the printer's business, and the panel does not pretend to override it.
Paper and orientation The size in millimetres, and the sentence that says what changes on the page because of it. "A4" as a word with no consequence. This panel's whole job is that a person choosing landscape knows nine columns will print instead of six.

Screen six — the audit of this mockup

Five families, four renderers, and the one sentence that explains all of it

What one language fixes, concretely

One paper defaultToday: A4 landscape in points for every PDF, an unnamed page for every print, the template's own millimetres for every banded run, and the browser's default for a ticket. Proposed: a family default, chosen in one place.
One accentCyan #00c0f4 / #22d3ee in three renderers at 2.1:1, crimson in none. Proposed: crimson, at weight.
One type floor6.8pt tiles, 7.5pt footers, 9px print CSS. Proposed: 8.5pt, everywhere, measured in points rather than pixels.
One letterheadThree renderers draw it and none of them says who generated the document. Proposed: one meta line, four facts, and the preparer's name in it.
One basis blockThe endpoint already carries basis, notes and dataQuality. It is printed by prose that the file's reader never sees. Proposed: the last sheet, and a switch to remove it.
One dead renderertablesToPdf — a landscape autoTable writer nothing calls any more. Proposed: delete it, because a second page layout that nobody uses is how the first one gets copied wrongly.

If this is built, in order

1The language as a module: paper sizes in millimetres, the margin, the grid, the type scale, the accent, and a basis-block component both renderers can draw. One file, not three.
2The generated document reads it: documentToPdf loses its hardcoded landscape, documentToPrintWindow gains an @page size, and the letterhead and running head come from the shared module.
3The pack: cover, contents, section openers and section-aware breaks on top of the same sections.
4The banded engine takes the accent and the type floor; the geometry is untouched, so no saved template moves a millimetre.
5The chooser, in both interfaces, replacing Print and Export. A schedule row gains the same four answers.
6The Help walkthrough and the configuration reference, because paper size and the basis block become things a person can set.

Each of those is a change to a renderer, and the renderers are separate enough that step 4 can ship without steps 1–3. That is the property the fragmentation accidentally bought, and it is worth keeping.
